        Your data stays here
        
      
        Everything is stored only in this browser (localStorage). There is no account, no server, and nothing is uploaded anywhere. Clearing this browser's site data deletes your log.
        
      
    
        
  
        

  
  
        
    
        About & how it was evaluated
        
    
        Not medical advice. DawaDiary does not diagnose, predict risk, detect disease, or recommend treatment. Talk to a doctor.
        
    
        What it does
        
    
          
      
        • Reminds you about medicines you already take, at the times you set.
          • 
      
        • Records taken / missed / skipped doses and shows adherence as taken ÷ scheduled — two ways, so pending doses are never silently dropped (see Evaluation).
          • 
      
        • Keeps a daily symptom journal (severity 0–10) and draws the daily mean with a least-squares trend line — descriptive statistics of your own log, nothing more.
          • 
      
        • Exports a plain-text summary and CSV files you can print or show a doctor.
          • 
      
        • No account, no server, no tracking. Data lives in your browser's localStorage.
          • 
    
        
    
        What it does not do
        
    
          
      
        • No diagnosis, no risk prediction, no disease detection, no dose advice.
          • 
      
        • No reminders when the browser is closed — this is a web page, not a phone alarm. Set a phone alarm for doses that must not be missed.
          • 
      
        • No medical accuracy claims of any kind.
          • 
    
        
    
        Evaluation (honest, reproducible)
        
    
        Full method and results: EVALUATION.md in the repository. In short:
        
    
          
      
        • Metric 1 — schedule & adherence arithmetic: on a scripted 14-day synthetic dataset (2 medicines × 2 doses/day = 56 scheduled doses, 50 marked taken), the app's adherence matched the hand-computed value exactly (89.3%).
          • 
      
        • Metric 2 — trend recovery: on synthetic symptom data rising exactly 0.5 severity points per day, the least-squares slope recovered was 0.50 points/day.
          • 
      
        • Metric 3 — unit tests: 20 unit tests, 20 scripted evaluation checks, and 12 Playwright UI smoke checks — all passing, including bad-input cases (empty names, invalid times, severity outside 0–10, unknown dose keys).
          • 
      
        • Where it stops being useful: the numbers are only as honest as the log. Missed entries, wrong times, or logging after the fact all bias adherence upward or downward; the app cannot detect that. The trend line needs at least 2 days with entries and says nothing about cause.
          • 
    
        
    
        Who benefits, who could be harmed
        
    
        Benefits: people who take regular medicines and want a simple, private record to show a doctor — especially where a clinic visit is short and a clear log saves time.
        
    
        Could be harmed if: someone treats the journal or the trend line as a diagnosis, delays seeing a doctor because "the app says nothing is wrong" (it never says that), or relies on a browser tab instead of a real alarm for a time-critical dose. The disclaimer on every screen and the absence of any diagnostic wording are the mitigations; they do not remove the risk.
        
    
        Built with
        
    
        Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No framework, no build step, no external requests, no analytics. Core logic is a dependency-free module (core.js) tested in Node.
